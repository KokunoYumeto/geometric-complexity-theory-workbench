# Counterexamples to the positivity and saturation hypotheses of GCT VI

WB-0015, version 1: [hexagon:2611.01046](https://hexagonmath.org/2611.01046), deposited 25 November 2026.

Part VI of Mulmuley's geometric complexity theory formulates three hypotheses on the stretching quasi-polynomials of Kronecker coefficients, plethysm coefficients and, more generally, multiplicities of subgroup restriction: the saturation index (SH) and the positivity index (PH2) should be bounded by a polynomial in the size of the input, and the generating function should admit a positive rational form whose largest denominator exponent, the modular index, is bounded by such a polynomial (PH3). We show that these hypotheses fail. For every n ≥ 2 the series Σ_{s≥0} g((sⁿ),(sⁿ),(sⁿ)) tˢ of triple-rectangular Kronecker coefficients has a pole at a primitive root of unity of order 2ⁿ⁻¹3ʳ for some r ≥ 0, so every positive rational form, indeed every expression P(t)/∏ᵢ(1 − t^{aᵢ})^{bᵢ}, has some aᵢ ≥ 2ⁿ⁻¹, while the input rank is n² + 5n; this refutes the existence clause of PH3 for Kronecker coefficients, also in the relaxed form that Mulmuley gave in the appendix to the paper of Briand, Orellana and Rosas. The proof reduces modulo three to the cyclic permutation of the three tensor factors, whose trace is the Hilbert series of the invariants of ℂⁿ ⊕ 𝕊_(2,1)ℂⁿ, and locates there a unique fixed curve of the grading group μ_{2ⁿ⁻¹}. For the plethysm coefficients of Sym^d(Sym³ ℂⁿ) along the ray of the determinant, the Hilbert series has simple poles at the primitive 2ⁿ⁻¹-th roots of unity for every n ≥ 4, which refutes PH3; a signed trace of these poles dominates all other terms, which gives positivity and saturation indices larger than 2^{cn} for every c < 1/4 and all large n, refuting PH2 and SH(a). Both arguments rest on a support theorem for cubic forms: a diagonal matrix of two-power order fixing a semistable cubic in n variables has determinant of order at most 2ⁿ⁻¹, with equality only for the chain cubic x₁²x₂ + ⋯ + x²_{n−1}xₙ + xₙ³. For restriction from a rank-one torus, which GCT VI admits as an explicit embedding, the three hypotheses fail at a fixed input rank.
